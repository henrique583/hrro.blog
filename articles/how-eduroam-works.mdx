## The Problem eduroam Solves

Universities want Wi-Fi that is:

- secure
- scalable
- usable by thousands of transient users
- interoperable across institutions

Traditional Wi-Fi setups (shared passwords, captive portals) don’t scale well and are insecure.

**eduroam** (education roaming) solves this by turning Wi-Fi access into an **identity-based authentication problem**, not a location-based one.

---

## High-Level Idea

eduroam works by separating:

- **where you connect** (the local campus network)
- **who you are** (your home institution identity)

When you connect to eduroam at *any* participating institution, that institution **does not authenticate you itself**.  
Instead, it securely forwards your credentials back to your **home university**, which vouches for you.

---

## The Role of 802.1X

eduroam uses the **IEEE 802.1X** standard for network access control.

This introduces three actors:

1. **Supplicant** – your device (laptop/phone)
2. **Authenticator** – the access point
3. **Authentication Server** – a RADIUS server

Your device never sends credentials directly to the access point.  
All authentication is handled through a secure, encrypted exchange.

---

## Authentication with RADIUS

The backbone of eduroam is **RADIUS (Remote Authentication Dial-In User Service)**.

Here’s the simplified flow:

1. You connect to the `eduroam` SSID.
2. Your device presents a username like:
    user@university.edu

3. The local RADIUS server sees the domain (`university.edu`).
4. It forwards the authentication request up the eduroam hierarchy.
5. The request eventually reaches your **home institution’s RADIUS server**.
6. Your university validates your credentials and sends back:
- **accept** or **reject**

The local network never learns your password.

---

## Why eduroam Is Secure

Several design choices make eduroam robust:

- **Mutual authentication**  
Your device verifies the server certificate before sending credentials.
- **Encrypted credential exchange**  
Authentication happens inside TLS (usually via EAP-TLS or PEAP).
- **No shared passwords**  
Each user authenticates individually.
- **Minimal trust**  
Visited institutions don’t need access to user databases.

In effect, eduroam treats Wi-Fi like a federated login system.

---

## Certificates and Trust

Your device trusts eduroam because:

- institutions deploy **X.509 certificates**
- certificate chains are validated during authentication
- misconfigured or fake access points fail verification

This prevents common attacks like evil-twin hotspots that steal credentials.

---

## Why eduroam Works Globally

eduroam succeeds because it is:

- **federated** – no single global user database
- **hierarchical** – national and regional RADIUS proxies
- **standards-based** – built on widely supported protocols

Each institution controls its own users, but agrees on shared rules and infrastructure.

---

## Key Takeaway

eduroam works by **decoupling identity from location**.

Your credentials never belong to the network you’re visiting — they belong to your home institution, which securely authenticates you anywhere in the world.

That’s why the same Wi-Fi name works at thousands of universities, airports, and research centers.
